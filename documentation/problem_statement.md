# Problem Statement

### Current situation
Globally, agriculture accounts for nearly 70% of all freshwater withdrawals. In many developing regions, irrigation is highly inefficient, relying on flood irrigation or routine-based scheduling. 

### Root cause
Farmers lack accessible, hyper-local data combining real-time soil moisture, crop stage water coefficients, and predictive weather intelligence. Decisions are made on visual intuition rather than data, leading to massive over-irrigation.

### Who is affected?
- **Farmers:** Face increased pumping costs and crop diseases from waterlogging.
- **Communities:** Suffer from depleting groundwater tables and water scarcity.
- **Environment:** Excessive water pumping consumes vast amounts of fossil fuel-generated electricity, contributing to CO2 emissions.

### Why existing solutions are insufficient
Current solutions are either highly fragmented (just a weather app, or just an expensive hardware soil sensor) or lack actionable AI. Generic weather apps don't translate rainfall into *crop-specific water requirements*. 

### Problem statement
"Farmers often irrigate based on routine rather than real-time crop and climate needs, leading to avoidable water loss, inflated energy costs, and reduced resilience, while climate variability increases agricultural uncertainty."
